    How this works

    Hand strength, strongest first

      A flush, three cards of the same suit, is its own rank. It beats every pair and every high card, and loses to a sequence, three tens or a trio. Two hands with the same ranks still split the pot unless one of them is a flush.

      Who actually wins

      The winner is decided by the hand ladder above, not by the percentage. The percentage only answers "how strong is this hand against a random one".

      The two are not identical. Comparing 741 real hands against each other (274,170 pairings), the percentage order disagrees with the actual result in 2,022 of them, and never by more than 0.21 percentage points. Only 18 of those cross two different hand types, so the numbers rank the types correctly: a flush always shows more than a pair, a pair more than a high card. Example: A-2-3 is a sequence at 96.15% that beats three tens at 96.35% in the real game, because a sequence outranks three tens.

      The odds are exact

      Hand strength checks all 18,424 hands an opponent could be dealt. Head to head checks all 15,180 hands the other player could hold from the 46 cards left once both hands are known. Nothing is sampled or estimated.
